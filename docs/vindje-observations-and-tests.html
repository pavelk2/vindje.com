Observations from the industry. From public sources. Company-reported and review-site figures are labelled, because neither is a representative sample.

  • Search matches keywords, not descriptionsIt needs the Dutch noun a seller typed. Attributes are optional, so size and included parts cannot be filtered. Marktplaats publishes anti-stuffing rules. [15]
  • Category share can move in four yearsReporting on CMA analysis puts Vinted at 50 to 60% of UK C2C apparel in 2025, from 5 to 10% in 2021, on free listing and one category. [25]
  • Sold prices are not published anywhereUnlike eBay, no Dutch platform shows what comparable items went for, and auction ads put an opening bid in the price position.
  • Fee incidence has shifted to the buyerMarktplaats charges the buyer for protection, Vinted charges sellers nothing, and eBay dropped UK private seller fees in 2024. [13][27][28]
  • Platform investment goes to sellerseBay now builds a listing from photographs alone. Buyer-side tooling across the industry is a keyword box and paid placement. [29]
  • Solved trust was solved operationallyAvito holds funds and moves goods. Zhuanzhuan closed unmediated C2C in 2025, citing disputes and margins, and kept inspection. [36][47]
  • Complaints concentrate on trust, not relevanceFraud surviving reports, protection charged either way, no route to a human, bans after registration. Self-selected sample. [9][10][11]
  • The engagement leaders are not search productsXianyu reports 209M monthly actives across 1.3M interest groups. Whatnot reports doubling volume on live auctions. [39][33]
  • Fraud is the buyer's main exposureCBS: purchase fraud hit 7% of people 15+ in 2024, up from 5.6% in 2022, ahead of every other online fraud type. [16]
  • A paid buyer-side layer already existsThree Dutch services sell faster listing alerts at €6.95 to €10.95 a month, and one advertises model-based filtering. [21][22][23]

The direction, and what vindje should stand for. A layer on supply that already exists, on the buyer's side of it. Three commitments, each with what it rules out.

One

Paid by the buyer, so it can answer to the buyer

Platform revenue comes from sellers, listings and the transaction, so no incumbent is paid to tell a buyer that nothing here fits.

Rules out seller tools, paid placement, and any fee that grows with transaction volume.

Two

Shows its work, every time

Each match states why it matched, each rejection is counted and grouped, and each price verdict shows how many comparable items it rests on.

Rules out silent ranking, undisclosed placement, and a number without the data behind it.

Three

Says nothing when it does not know

Fraud is the buyer's main worry in this market, so a confident wrong price or a jumpy risk flag costs more than an empty state.

Rules out filling a thin comparable set, or a missing attribute, with a plausible guess.

Not a marketplace, not an escrow, not a courier, not a seller tool. Bracketed numbers are sources on page two. 1 of 2

Ten hypotheses, the test for each, and the rule that settles it

Ten hypotheses to test. Each states its test and the result that kills it. Thresholds are judgement calls, sized to be decidable in weeks at current traffic.

HypothesisHow it is testedFalsified if
H1Match quality is already good enough to build on50 hand-labelled wishes across five categories, run against one frozen listing snapshot, reporting precision, recall and cost per searchPrecision below 70%
H2Constraint-rich wishes are where the value sitsParse and store the constraint count per search, then compare listing views per search across constraint bands over four weeks of live trafficNo positive relationship
H3The stated reason is what makes a result usableEqual-split A/B, reason line shown against hidden, decided at a sample fixed before the test startsNo positive difference
H4Disclosing what was hidden builds trust rather than doubtA/B on the "43 listings hidden, here is why" panel, measured on 7 day return rate, not on clicksThe disclosure arm returns less
H5A saved search turns a one-time visit into a habitOffer a saved search at the end of results. Measure creation rate, then 7 day return for creators against one-time searchersCreation under 10%
H6Buyers will pay for a saved searchFree tier of two searches at 15 minute checks, paid tier at the price the market already charges. Measure conversion among users with an active search over three weeksConversion under 2%
H7A price verdict changes what buyers doA/B verdict against no verdict on seller-contact clicks, plus one in-product question to users who saw it. Direction is uncertain: a calibrated verdict should also prevent some purchasesNeither contacts nor stated decisions move
H8Risk flags can be made accurate enough to publishRun rules plus model inspection over listings users reported as suspicious and over a hand-labelled clean set. Report recall and false positives separatelyFalse positives above 20%
H9Buyers who do not write Dutch are a distinct segmentSplit engagement and 30 day retention by input language, and interview five non-Dutch speakers to read the quantitative resultThe two groups behave alike
H10Dutch long-tail pages acquire buyers at a workable costPublish 200 constraint pages and 25 valuation pages, measure organic sessions and saved-search conversion at day 90, with a €500 category-intent ads test as the comparisonUnder 1,000 sessions a month, or over €3 per saved search
